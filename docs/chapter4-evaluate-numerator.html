Evaluating the numerator of Bayes' rule at one w

Chapter 4 · For the binary dataset, pick a weight vector w and compute g(w) = prior × likelihood, the numerator of the posterior. Step 1 is the prior, which needs only w and σ². Step 2 is the likelihood, where each point gets the probability the model assigns to its observed label and the 40 numbers are multiplied.

Step 1: the prior

The prior is N(0, σ²I) on the two weights, so with D = 2: p(w) = (2πσ²)−1 exp(−wᵀw / (2σ²)). It depends only on the weights and says nothing about the data. The rings are 1, 2 and 3 prior standard deviations from the centre; the red dot is the chosen w.

Step 2: the likelihood

For each point: z = wᵀx, then P = 1/(1 + e−z) is the probability of label 1, and the factor in the likelihood is P if t = 1 and 1 − P if t = 0.

Each point is coloured by P(observed label): green when high, red when low. The line is the boundary wᵀx = 0.

Likelihood = product of the P(observed t) column
log-likelihood = sum of the logs

The product of 40 numbers below 1 can get extremely small, so in practice we add logs instead of multiplying probabilities.

Step 3: multiply (or add the logs)

g(w) = likelihood × prior
log g(w) = log-likelihood + log prior

g(w) is proportional to the posterior density at w. It is not the posterior itself, because we have not divided by Z, the integral of g over all w, which is the part with no closed form. Try different w: g is largest near the weights that fit the data and are not too large.
